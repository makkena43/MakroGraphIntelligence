# Multibagger-process research: two missed-winner audits

4 October 2026. Research only; no software changes or current buy recommendations. Bounded follow-up to the six-company pilot. Original assessments and returns remain unchanged.

## Design and limitations

Examine CEAT and GIPCL because the prior pilot left them on WAIT despite subsequent strong returns. This is explicitly an outcome-selected error analysis, not an independent validation or unbiased success-rate estimate. Dates below are defensible research-update milestones found within the reviewed documents, not claims of the earliest possible market signal. No updated full underwriting or purchase decision was completed. A research alert is not a retrospectively executable buy strategy.

## CEAT: recovery became more measurable

- 20 July 2022: June-quarter revenue ₹2,818cr, EBITDA margin 6.1%, PAT ₹9cr. Demand/capacity ramp improving, but commodity inflation hurt margins. Management noted recent commodity corrections conditionally.
- 7 November 2022: September-quarter revenue ₹2,894cr, margin 7%, PAT ₹6.4cr. Price adjustments and stabilizing input costs supported the outlook, but consolidated net debt rose ₹197cr. This was mixed evidence, not a clean all-clear.
- 25 January 2023: December-quarter revenue ₹2,727cr, EBITDA ₹231cr and PAT approximately ₹35cr. Inventory reduction ₹280cr and another sequential margin improvement supported a research upgrade. Revenue declined sequentially, export uncertainty remained, and capex was still approximately ₹900cr for the year.
- Data-quality warning: the January press release says consolidated margin 13%, inconsistent with its own EBITDA/revenue and stated 144bp sequential improvement. ₹231/₹2,727 implies approximately 8.47%, not 13%. The filed result separately confirms revenue ₹2,727.20cr and PAT ₹34.85cr. Do not blindly summarize the headline percentage.
- Interpretation: two successive operating-margin improvements plus working-capital release were more informative than merely waiting for large year-on-year profit growth. This justified fresh valuation work; it did not by itself establish an attractive entry price.

Sources: [July release](https://archives.nseindia.com/corporate/CEATLTD_20072022184739_CEAT_SELetter_PressRelease_20Jul22.pdf), [November release](https://archives.nseindia.com/corporate/CEATLTD_07112022181603_CEAT_SELetter_PressRelease_7Nov22.pdf), [January release](https://archives.nseindia.com/corporate/CEATLTD_25012023183433_CEAT_SELetter_PressRelease_25Jan23.pdf), [January financial statement](https://archives.nseindia.com/corporate/CEAT_SEletter_BMOutcome_25Jan23_finalforfiling_25012023173851.pdf).

## GIPCL: project aspirations became more concrete

- November 2022 call, transcript filed 9 November: park development and owned generation were distinct. Park capex approximately ₹1,100cr; management discussed 30% subsidy and approximately 13% equity returns after subsidy. Generating projects contemplated 70:30 debt/equity. Financing discussions were underway, not fully tied up; future generation revenue still depended on tariff/PPA.
- June 2023 call, transcript filed 20 June: management described a 600MW owned solar project with GUVNL letter of intent, prospective ₹2.73/unit PPA, approximately ₹3,600cr project cost, approximately ₹430cr eventual annual revenue and roughly 12% post-tax equity IRR. Park capex remained separate. Project subsidy should not be inferred from park subsidy.
- Timeline discrepancy: the transcript refers to November 2024 and later an analyst refers to November 2025; commissioning timing needs reconciliation rather than silently choosing the favorable date. The transcript described PPA execution as prospective, not completed.
- Interpretation: an award with identified purchaser and tentative economics is a meaningful upgrade from generic renewable ambition. However, roughly 12% project equity IRR does not establish a high-return compounder or predict stock returns. Purchase valuation, debt terms, cost overruns and project completion still determine attractiveness.

Sources: [November transcript](https://archives.nseindia.com/corporate/GIPCL_09112022115742_SE_AnalystMeetTranscript_09112022.pdf), [June transcript](https://archives.nseindia.com/corporate/GIPCL_20062023105342_SE_ConcallTranscript_20062023f.pdf).

## Research implications, not retroactive rule optimization

1. Separate durable reinvestment-led compounders from finite recovery/revaluation investments. Both can double, but their holding and exit logic differs. Prioritize the former for the user's low-maintenance, multi-year objective without assuming this pilot found one.
2. Keep WAIT companies in a quarterly evidence-change review. For recovery: margins, physical demand and working capital. For expansion: customer award/qualification, funding, unit economics and commissioning. The examples motivate prospective tests; they do not establish a universal two-quarter rule.
3. Require a bridge from sales to sustainable diluted EPS, cash generation and incremental returns on capital. Do not equate orders, capacity, consolidated revenue or a project IRR with shareholder return.
4. Stress valuation without multiple expansion. Price multiple approximately equals EPS growth factor times terminal P/E divided by entry P/E. With 25% annual EPS growth for five years, constant valuation produces 3.05x, while a fall from 30x to 20x produces only 2.03x. These are mathematical scenarios, not expected returns. Growth must be feasible and financed, not selected to reach a target.
5. Keep failure cases and expose misses. Any revised rule needs a frozen, unselected cohort or prospective paper watchlist. This two-winner audit cannot estimate precision, false-positive rate or alpha.

Bounded round complete. No automation, new universe scan, or continuous monitoring initiated.
